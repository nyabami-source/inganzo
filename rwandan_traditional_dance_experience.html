Rwandan Traditional Dance Styles

Each Rwandan dance style tells an ancient story of pride, nobility, nature, and community joy passed through generations.

Warriors' Dance

Intore (The Chosen Ones)

Historically performed by elite royal palace warriors. Features energetic high-jump leaps, intricate footwork, and wielding of spears (*Itshuri*) and decorative hair wear (*Umushatsi*).

High Energy • Powerful Athleticism • Martial Precision
Maidens' Grace

Umushayizero & Amaraba

Performed by women with supreme poise and elegance. Mimics the majestic stride of the long-horned royal cattle (*Inyambo*) and the serene arm movements of the Grey Crowned Crane (*Umusambi*).

Gentle Swaying • Crowned Crane Movements • Royal Elegance
Harvest Folk Dance

Ikinimba

Originating from northern Rwanda (Musanze & Nyabihu). A highly rhythmic harvest dance celebrating agricultural yield, community resilience, and unity, accompanied by acoustic *Inanga*.

Harvest Joy • Heavy Rhythm Stomping • Community Spirit
Royal Percussion

Ingoma Drum Spectacle

The heartbeat of Rwanda. Master drummers move in dramatic synchronization around the central master drum (*Kalinga*), combining acrobatics, dynamic shouting, and thunderous rhythms.

Polyrhythmic Thunder • Synchronized Choreography

Craftsmanship & Regalia

Instruments & Traditional Attire

Ingoma (Sacred Drum)

Carved from indigenous tree trunks (*Ishoza* or *Umugumu*) and covered with cattle hide. Symbolizes national sovereignty and unity.

Percussion

Inanga (Trough Zither)

A flat carved wooden soundboard with 6 to 12 strings. Produces soothing, meditative melodies for storytelling and epic poetry.

Stringed Zither

Umuduri (Musical Bow)

A flexible wooden bow with a wire string and a dried gourd resonator (*Inyenga*). Struck with a stick while shaking a rattle (*Inseke*).

Single String Bow

Ikembe (Lamellophone)

Handheld wooden soundbox with tuned metal strips plucked by thumbs. Creates polyphonic rhythmic patterns.

Thumb Piano

Umushanana

The ceremonial dress for women. Consists of a flowing wrap skirt (*Inshabure*) paired with a sash draped elegantly over one shoulder.

Women's Ceremonial

Amasunzu Hairstyle

An iconic crest-like traditional Rwandan hairstyle carved into elaborate crescent shapes symbolizing status, prestige, and pride.

Sculpted Hair Art

Umushatsi & Headband

Flowing mane headpieces worn by Intore dancers made from sisal fibers, dynamic shaking creates dramatic visual motion during leaps.

Warrior Headpiece

Itshuri & Ingabo

Traditional light wooden spears (*Itshuri*) and decorated shields (*Ingabo*) carried during warrior dance routines.

Umushayizero Technique

The Crane Wing Extension

Arms are gracefully stretched outward like wings, curving gently at the elbows. Body sways softly in unison with smooth foot steps, honoring the majestic Grey Crowned Crane (*Umusambi*).

Key Instruction Checklist:
  • Keep shoulders relaxed and neck elongated.
  • Extend arms smoothly without rigid tension.
  • Step lightly on the balls of your feet in rhythm.
